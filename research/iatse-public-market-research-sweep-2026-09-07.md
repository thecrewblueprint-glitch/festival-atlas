# IATSE public institutional and labor-market research sweep

**Research date:** 2026-09-07  
**Primary scope:** United States; Canada appears only where needed to describe IATSE International  
**Evidence standard:** current public primary sources from IATSE International, the IATSE Training Trust Fund, and official local-union websites  
**Publication status:** public-safe research; no personal information retained

## Executive finding

Production Atlas should model IATSE as its own labor-organization ecosystem, not as a conventional employer, vendor, job board, certification body, or single national hiring funnel.

The useful relationship is:

`IATSE International -> district -> autonomous local union -> market/craft jurisdiction -> agreement or referral mechanism -> employer -> work call`

These states are related but not equivalent:

- discovering a potentially relevant local;
- registering for a referral, hiring-hall, extra, overflow, probationary, replacement, apprenticeship, or other local mechanism;
- receiving a referral;
- being employed for a call;
- becoming a member;
- accessing a training program;
- holding a credential or satisfying a specific call requirement;
- working under a touring agreement.

The [IATSE Join page](https://iatse.net/join/) identifies two broad national routes: apply to an existing local under that local's criteria, or organize a workplace with coworkers. It also expressly says the organizer form is not an application for work or membership. The official local sources reviewed for this sweep show materially different entry mechanisms. There is no evidence for one safe national five-step formula from “overhire” to membership.

## Institutional structure

The [IATSE About page](https://iatse.net/about/) describes an international labor organization founded in 1893 whose represented work spans live theater, motion-picture and television production, trade shows and exhibitions, broadcasting, concerts, and supporting shops.

The same source states that:

- more than 360 local unions operate across the United States and Canada;
- locals are organized by geography and craft jurisdiction;
- each local is an autonomous, independent labor organization;
- locals determine their own bylaws, elections, dues, meetings, and internal rules;
- locals negotiate agreements covering wages, work rules, and grievances;
- some locals administer benefits and training;
- IATSE International supports locals through nationwide agreements, education, legislative work, organizing, and bargaining.

The current Join page displayed “more than 170,000” entertainment professionals on the research date, while the About page still displayed “more than 168,000.” This report retains that first-party mismatch rather than manufacturing one exact count.

### Atlas entity treatment

| Layer | Public role | Critical boundary |
|---|---|---|
| IATSE International | International organization, national agreements, education, organizing, bargaining, and directory | Not a national job application |
| District | Regional coordination among locals | Navigation only; not a jurisdiction ruling |
| Local union | Autonomous organization with geographic and/or craft jurisdiction | Local rules and routes require current verification |
| Agreement | Bounded relationship covering employers, classifications, terms, and time | Do not flatten into a permanent national rule |
| Referral or hiring hall | May connect eligible workers to employers | Registration, referral, membership, employment, and guaranteed work are separate |
| Employer | Employs and pays workers for a call or position under the applicable arrangement | Do not infer employer-of-record from a local listing |
| Training provider | Offers or supports training under eligibility rules | Availability does not establish a universal requirement |
| Touring mechanism | Coordinates touring agreement and local labor needs | Not a general job board |

The strongest direct example of the referral/employer distinction is [IATSE Local 27](https://www.iatse27.com/), whose public site says the local is a referral hall and does not employ stagehands. That finding is evidence about Local 27; it is not generalized into a legal conclusion about every local.

## Departments and career sectors

The official directory uses overlapping department filters. On the research date it displayed approximately 305 U.S. entries and filter counts of 28 Broadcast, 56 Motion Picture and Television, 279 Stagecraft, and 17 Trade Show. The filter counts must not be added because one organization can appear under more than one department.

- [Stagecraft](https://iatse.net/stagecraft/) covers theaters, opera, dance, arenas, concert halls, stadiums, parks, and related performance settings. It is central to festival work but not a synonym for every IATSE organization or live-event role.
- [Trade Show](https://iatse.net/tradeshow/) covers convention, exhibition, meeting, display, and decorator work involving installation, operation, and dismantling.
- [Broadcast](https://iatse.net/broadcast/) covers station and live-sports production roles including technical direction, audio, camera, video, replay, editing, graphics, and utility work.
- [Motion Picture and Television](https://iatse.net/mptv/) includes production crafts that can share skills with live events while operating through different locals, rosters, agreements, and hiring structures.

### Safe public workflow

Production Atlas can safely advise a worker to:

1. identify both craft and geography;
2. use the current official directory to find potentially relevant organizations;
3. read the local's current work/referral and membership pages separately;
4. identify whether the public route is a referral list, hiring hall, extra/overflow list, apprenticeship, workplace-organizing path, direct-employer posting, or another mechanism;
5. verify current requirements, availability, skills evidence, costs, and membership criteria through official sources;
6. treat a referral as separate from an offer, employment relationship, or guaranteed schedule.

Production Atlas must not claim that:

- most workers nationally start as permit hands;
- every local keeps an overhire list;
- working calls automatically creates membership eligibility;
- sponsorship, a vote, or a particular hour threshold is universal;
- one local's dispatch vocabulary applies nationally;
- an organizer form is a job application;
- a local is necessarily the employer.

## Training and credentials

The [IATSE Training Trust Fund](https://www.iatsetrainingtrust.org/) is a labor-management training organization involving IATSE and signatory employers. It offers or supports safety, craft, OSHA, AV, and other training resources. Its [certification reimbursement page](https://www.iatsetrainingtrust.org/certification-reimbursement) lists eligible categories under current program rules and funding.

That evidence does not show that every credential is required by IATSE, every local, or every call. Production Atlas must model these separately:

`training opportunity -> provider -> eligibility -> local/employer/venue/call requirement observation -> effective date`

Training availability, reimbursement eligibility, course completion, certification, referral eligibility, and employer authorization are different facts.

## Touring

The official [Introduction to Pink Contract Touring](https://iatse.net/about/stagecraft/touring-2/) describes Pink Contracts as collective bargaining agreements between IATSE and live-entertainment producers. Multiple agreements and tiers exist. The [Yellow Cards page](https://iatse.net/yellow-cards/) publishes touring show and labor-requirement information.

Atlas boundaries:

- a Yellow Card is not a general job posting;
- a Pink Contract is not one uniform nationwide rate or condition schedule;
- a touring position and a local referral are different routes;
- no event-specific staffing relationship should be inferred merely because a tour or venue is union-associated.

## Directory audit

The retained `data/iatse-us-local-directory.js` snapshot is dated 2026-06-21 and contains 221 records. The official directory displayed 305 U.S. entries during the final check; an earlier same-day retrieval displayed 306. The official state filters also exposed locals in Delaware, Idaho, New Hampshire, North Dakota, and South Dakota, which the retained snapshot does not represent.

The retained snapshot is therefore a useful initial index but is not exhaustive. The current official directory remains canonical for local discovery. A future complete refresh needs a reproducible source process, record-level observation dates, official multi-department tags, deduplication, and count reconciliation before the product can claim national completeness.

The parser was also corrected so `Washington, DC` maps to the `District of Columbia` state field.

## Public-data and privacy boundary

Public packages may retain:

- organization name and local number;
- public jurisdiction and craft descriptions;
- official organization or directory URLs;
- organization-level public route descriptions;
- source and retrieval dates;
- non-personal market and mechanism facts.

Public packages must not retain:

- names of officers, dispatchers, applicants, or workers;
- personal email addresses or direct phone numbers;
- worker histories, anecdotes, inferred identities, or application data;
- private referral or availability status;
- member-only material;
- pay, lodging, private call, or other sensitive operational details under the current Atlas policy.

Some official pages displayed personal-detail-bearing material. Those values were unnecessary to this analysis and were not transcribed or retained.

## Product boundary

Production Atlas owns this public-safe, time-sensitive labor-market and institutional research. The Crew Blueprint owns durable technical, safety, academic, and job-readiness instruction and should link to Atlas rather than copy volatile local rules into course content.

A Crew Blueprint course cannot imply that it grants IATSE membership, places a learner on a referral list, satisfies a local requirement, substitutes for authorized third-party training, qualifies a learner for specialist work, or carries IATSE endorsement unless a formal relationship says so.

## Research limits

- This is public-source research, not legal advice, a jurisdiction determination, union endorsement, referral, or employment offer.
- The local sample demonstrates variation; it does not estimate national prevalence.
- The official directory is dynamic, and same-day count observations differed by one.
- No member-only material was accessed.
- No claim was built from worker reviews, social posts, forums, or salary aggregators.
- No personal data values were retained.
- Local rules, intake capacity, training availability, agreement coverage, and touring records require continued revalidation.
